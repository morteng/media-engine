# Capybara Research Notes

This document collects verified facts about capybaras (Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris) to demonstrate Media Engine's provenance and claim tracking features.

## Research Methodology

Facts were gathered from reputable sources including:
- Natural History Museum (London)
- The Nature Conservancy
- World Wildlife Fund
- Scientific publications

Each fact in the corresponding chapter includes a claim ID that can be verified against this research document.

## Primary Sources

### Natural History Museum (London)
**URL**: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/things-you-might-not-know-about-capybaras.html
**Accessed**: 2025-12-20
**Reliability**: High (institutional source)

Key facts extracted:
- Greater capybara adults grow up to 1.3 metres in length
- Greater capybara adults weigh over 60 kilogrammes
- Lesser capybara weighs up to 28 kg, measures about 1 metre
- Females give birth to 2-8 pups
- Can hold breath underwater for up to 5 minutes
- Wild herds contain 10-20 individuals
- Groups expand to ~30 during dry season

### The Nature Conservancy
**URL**: https://blog.nature.org/2020/02/11/capybara-meet-the-worlds-largest-rodent/
**Accessed**: 2025-12-20
**Reliability**: High (conservation organization)

Key facts extracted:
- Can weigh up to 140 pounds (63.5 kg)
- Mating occurs underwater for approximately 6 seconds
- Herds can number in the hundreds
- Practice coprophagia for nutrient extraction

### World Wildlife Fund
**URL**: https://www.worldwildlife.org/stories/are-capybaras-rodents-and-5-other-capybara-facts
**Accessed**: 2025-12-20
**Reliability**: High (conservation organization)

Key facts referenced:
- Capybaras play important ecosystem roles
- Related to guinea pigs and rock cavies
- Semi-aquatic lifestyle adaptations
