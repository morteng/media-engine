# Fun Facts About Capybaras

*A demonstration of Media Engine's provenance and fact-tracking features, featuring the world's most lovable giant rodent.*

## Meet the Capybara

The **capybara** (*Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris*) holds the distinguished title of being the world's largest living rodent. [CAPY-001] Native to South America, these gentle giants have captured hearts worldwide with their calm demeanor and surprisingly social nature.

## Size Matters

Adult capybaras are impressively large:

- **Length**: Up to 1.3 metres (4.3 feet)
- **Weight**: Over 60 kilogrammes (132 pounds)
- **Comparison**: About the size of a Labrador retriever

[CAPY-002] Female greater capybaras are slightly larger than males, which is unusual among mammals.

## Aquatic Superstars

Capybaras are semi-aquatic and supremely adapted to life in and around water:

### Built for Swimming
- **Webbed feet**: Partially webbed toes help them paddle efficiently
- **Strategic anatomy**: Eyes, ears, and nostrils are positioned high on their heads
- **Breath-holding**: Can remain completely submerged for up to 5 minutes [CAPY-003]

### Water Romance
Here's a fun fact: capybaras mate exclusively in water. If a female isn't interested in a particular male, she simply submerges or swims away. The actual mating lasts only about 6 seconds.

## Social Butterflies

Capybaras are highly social creatures:

| Group Size | Condition |
|------------|-----------|
| 10-20 | Normal herd size |
| 30+ | Dry season gatherings |
| 100+ | Observed in exceptional cases |

### Communication
They're surprisingly vocal, with a repertoire that includes:
- Purring (contentment)
- Barking (alarm)
- Whistling (communication)
- Teeth-chattering (submission)

## The Unusual Diet Secret

Capybaras practice **coprophagia** - they eat their own feces. Before you say "ew," here's why it's actually clever:

1. Their diet of tough grasses is hard to digest
2. The first pass through creates nutrient-rich, bacteria-laden pellets
3. Eating these pellets extracts remaining nutrients
4. It also replenishes beneficial gut bacteria

It's essentially a biological recycling system.

## Nature's Living Furniture

Capybaras have earned nicknames like "nature's ottoman" and "moving chair" because other animals love to sit on them:

- Birds perch on their backs
- Monkeys ride along
- Turtles climb aboard
- Even baby crocodiles have been spotted hitching rides

Their calm temperament makes them the perfect living platform.

## Family Life

- **Litter size**: 2-8 pups
- **Precocial young**: Pups can stand, walk, and swim shortly after birth
- **Nursing period**: About 3 months
- **Independence**: Young stay with the group for about 1 year

## Conservation Status

Capybaras are currently listed as **Least Concern** by the IUCN. However, they face threats from:
- Habitat loss due to agricultural expansion
- Hunting for meat and leather in some regions
- Water pollution affecting their wetland habitats
